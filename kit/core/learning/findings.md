# Findings and their lessons

Something a person, a reviewer, a client or a check found wrong in the work, and
what it taught. **Not a to-do list:** whether it is fixed lives wherever the work
lives. This file holds the one thing that does not — *why the loop let it
through, and where the lesson now lives.*

Procedure: `procedures/learn-from-finding.md`. Counted by `scripts/retro-evidence.sh`.

## Gap — which part of the loop let it through

| Gap | Meaning | The lesson goes to |
|---|---|---|
| `standard` | nothing said what good is | a decision, rubric or foundation — decided by the person, never guessed |
| `check` | the standard existed; nothing checked it | a check in the procedure, or a gate |
| `render` | only visible in the finished thing, as the recipient sees it | a check on the finished output, or the [human lane](../contracts/handoff.md) |
| `judgement` | a person's call — "feels off", "not us" | a judged example in `corpus/` with its reason, a decision, or the [human lane](../contracts/handoff.md) |

The **human lane** is the harness's standing list of what its checks cannot reach —
what it must contain, who owns it, and when a line leaves it are in
[`contracts/handoff.md`](../contracts/handoff.md). Routing a lesson there is an
answer, not a shrug, only while that list is named and owned.

**Standard** names what was broken — a decision's date, a rubric, a foundation
file — or `none`. Use the same name every time: that column is how a recurrence
is seen.

**A standard broken on two different days is a recurrence.** The first lesson
did not hold. Climb a rung — journal → memory → rubric or procedure step → gate —
and never record the same lesson twice.
